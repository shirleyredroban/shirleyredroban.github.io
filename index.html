Hi everyone, and thanks for stopping by!

I'm a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at the University of Maryland and a Research Fellow in the Research Department at the Inter-American Development Bank.

My research focuses on labor economics and the economics of education. On the teacher side, I study how public programs built on financial incentives shape who is drawn into teaching, and how violence affects teachers' decisions to stay. On the student side, I study the mechanisms behind peer interactions, and how industry certifications affect the long-term outcomes of disadvantaged students. My work uses experimental and quasi-experimental methods, with data from Chile, Mexico, Ecuador, and the United States.

Main paperPaper principal

The Effects of Violence on Teacher Attrition

Teacher attrition remains a major concern in Texas: more than 12 percent of teachers left the classroom in 2023–24, up from roughly 10 percent before the pandemic, and these exits are concentrated among early-career and non-certified teachers. This paper examines how exposure to violence, both inside schools and in the neighborhoods around them, shapes two outcomes: leaving the profession and moving between schools within the Texas public school system.

I combine linked statewide administrative records from 2008 onward with geocoded measures of neighborhood violence, following teachers over time across schools and districts. Comparing changes within schools over time, I estimate how shifts in exposure to violence relate to teachers' decisions to exit or transfer. I also study whether violence-related turnover reaches students, through greater exposure to inexperienced or non-certified teachers and through their later academic performance. The results inform policy debates on teacher retention, school safety, and the distribution of educational resources.

La deserción docente sigue siendo una preocupación central en Texas: más del 12 por ciento de los docentes dejó las aulas en 2023–24, frente a cerca del 10 por ciento antes de la pandemia, y estas salidas se concentran entre docentes al inicio de su carrera y sin certificación. Este trabajo estudia cómo la exposición a la violencia, tanto dentro de las escuelas como en los barrios que las rodean, afecta dos decisiones: abandonar la profesión y cambiarse de escuela dentro del sistema público de Texas.

Combino registros administrativos estatales vinculados desde 2008 con medidas georreferenciadas de violencia en los barrios, y sigo a los docentes a lo largo del tiempo entre escuelas y distritos. Comparando cambios dentro de una misma escuela en el tiempo, estimo cómo las variaciones en la exposición a la violencia se relacionan con la decisión de salir o trasladarse. También analizo si esta rotación llega a los estudiantes, a través de una mayor exposición a docentes sin experiencia o sin certificación y de su rendimiento académico posterior. Los resultados aportan evidencia para el debate sobre retención docente, seguridad escolar y distribución de recursos educativos.
